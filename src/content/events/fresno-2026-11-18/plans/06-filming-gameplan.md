# Filming Gameplan — Fresno dinner, Wednesday, Nov. 18

**Purpose:** leave Fresno with a library of professional footage and photos Sundae can use for
months: company social accounts, the website, the Membership VSL and paid ads. One dinner should
produce **40+ short clips, one full talk, one highlight film, eight to 12 interview soundbites and
150+ edited photos.**

> **Venue and start time are to be announced.** Everything below that doesn't depend on the room
> is ready now (crew, audio, settings, interviews, releases, turnaround). Section 4 (positions)
> and section 5 (minute-by-minute) are written as a template; finish them after a walk-through of
> the confirmed venue, and get the venue's written OK to film.

**Kit (as planned for Sundae's dinners):** one or two cameras plus a phone as an extra vertical
angle. Two wireless lav mics, each paired to one camera, **both clipped to Josh's shirt**, while
Josh speaks into the venue's handheld mic. Every camera records clean, close voice audio, and the
room hears Josh through the PA.

---

## 1. Roles (minimum crew: two people, ideal: three)

| Role | Who | Owns |
|---|---|---|
| **A-Cam operator** | Videographer | Josh's talk (locked off, tripod), main interviews |
| **B-Cam / photo operator** | Second shooter | Roaming: guests, details, reactions, photos (hybrid camera) |

Book a Fresno-based crew if possible (no travel day). Send them this plan and the venue's floor
plan once confirmed.

## 2. Audio plan (the most important part)

- **Lav 1 → A-Cam, Lav 2 → B-Cam.** Clip both on Josh's shirt placket, 6 to 8 inches below the
  chin, about an inch apart. Hide the cables, mic heads facing up; add foam windscreens if any part
  of the evening is outdoors. Tape the transmitters to a belt or back pocket.
- **Handheld PA mic:** chest height, slightly off-axis. The lavs pick up Josh's real voice; the PA
  is for the room. Keep the PA speakers **in front of and away from** Josh so the lavs don't pick
  up delayed PA sound (echo). Ask the venue's AV for one speaker per side, aimed at the tables.
- **Levels:** normal speech peaks around **-12 dB**, loud laughs under **-6 dB**. Use the 32-bit
  float or safety track if the mics offer it, and the transmitters' **onboard recording** as a
  backup.
- **Camera audio:** Ch1 = lav, Ch2 = the camera's onboard mic (room tone and applause for the edit).
- **Sync:** one hand clap on camera before the talk, plus timecode if available. Record **30
  seconds of room tone** before guests arrive.
- **Interviews:** move Lav 2 to the guest; Josh's lav stays on A-Cam for Josh's interviews.
- **Fresh batteries** in every transmitter and receiver before doors and again before dinner
  service. Carry spares.

## 3. Camera settings (an evening event; adjust after the walk-through)

| | A-Cam (talk) | B-Cam (roam) | Phone |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resolution | 4K | 4K | 4K vertical |
| Frame rate | 24 or 30 fps (match all cameras) | 30 + 60 fps for slow-motion details | 30 fps |
| Shutter | 1/50 (24p) or 1/60 (30p) | double the frame rate | auto (lock exposure) |
| Profile | Log or flat if a colorist will grade; otherwise standard + low contrast | same as A-Cam | HDR off for consistency |
| White balance | **Manual**, set to the room's lights at the walk-through, then locked | same as A-Cam | lock |
| Lens | 24-70 mm on a tripod | 35 mm and 85 mm primes, f/1.4-2.0 | 1× and 2× |

**Light Josh.** Restaurant and event lighting rarely lights faces well. Put one battery LED panel
with a softbox (bi-color, matched to the room) 45° off Josh's speaking spot, 6 to 8 feet up, plus
a small fill or bounce on the other side. Frame Josh with the screen showing "Today's Outlook"
visible over a shoulder. That's the money frame.

## 4. Camera positions (template — finish at the walk-through)

- **Front:** Josh's speaking spot with the screen on one side and the LED key at 45° on the other.
  Choose the backdrop at the walk-through (a clean wall, the room's best feature, or Sundae
  signage); avoid windows behind Josh.
- **Floor:** guest tables in front of the speaking spot, with a B-Cam roam lane along each side.
- **Back:** A-Cam on a tripod at center-back, raised above head height, 24-70 mm at about 50 mm.
  One continuous medium shot of Josh with the screen; it never stops during the talk.
- **Side aisle:** the phone, shooting vertical, framed for Reels (top third clear for captions).
- **B-Cam** alternates a tight close-up of Josh from a 30-45° side angle with audience reactions
  (nods, note-taking, laughter), eight to 10 seconds a shot.
- **High angle:** if the venue has a balcony, mezzanine or stairs, use it for the room-wide and
  dinner overheads.

## 6. The interview station (testimonials and soundbites)

The producer sets it up during dessert in a quiet, well-lit spot; the cameras move to it right
after the toast and run it through the wrap-up as guests leave. Line up six to eight guests
(attendees, members if any, partners) and Josh. Each gets a one- to two-minute interview, A-Cam
medium + B-Cam close. **Get a signed release first.** If the wrap-up is short, record Josh's
answers during setup instead and keep the station for guests.

**Questions for guests (operators):**
1. Tell us who you are and what you do. ("I'm ___, I run ___ in ___.")
2. What's the hardest part of acquisitions in the Central Valley right now?
3. What was the most useful thing you heard tonight?
4. How would you describe the room tonight?
5. In one sentence: why would an operator come to one of these dinners?

**Questions for Josh (10-15 answers, 20-40 seconds each, looking just off-camera):**
1. Why did you start Sundae?
2. Why Fresno, and why now?
3. What's changed in the market this year, and what hasn't?
4. What does a strong Sundae member look like?
5. What does Sundae bring to an operator that they can't build alone?
6. Why dinners instead of webinars?
7. What would you tell an operator who's doing 20 deals a year and feels stuck?

**Never** prompt or film earnings or income claims. If a guest volunteers numbers, flag the clip
for legal review before use (FDD Item 19 rules).

## 7. Photo plan (150+ keepers)

- **Must-get list:** the room wide (empty and full) · the venue's exterior at dusk · Josh speaking
  (wide, medium, tight, with the screen) · Josh with guests (five or more) · table details · food
  and drinks · the toast · QR card in hand · group photo · Victoria and team · Sundae signage.
- Shoot RAW. Deliver 30 hero edits by noon Thursday, Nov. 19, and the full gallery within 72 hours.

## 8. Releases, consent and rights

- **Signage at check-in:** "This event is being photographed and filmed for Sundae marketing. Let
  us know if you'd prefer not to appear."
- **Signed appearance releases** for every interview (QR to a simple release form on the producer's
  tablet). Give "no-photo" guests a colored wristband or name-badge sticker, and keep that list for
  the photo selection.
- **Venue:** confirm filming in writing with the venue's events team. Staff and other guests are
  not featured without consent. Who owns the photos (Sundae's photographer) is agreed before the
  night.
- **Music:** licensed tracks only in paid ads (Instagram's in-app music isn't licensed for ads).

## 9. Data management (never lose a shot)

- Name cards A1, A2, B1… Offload at the dinner-service break and after the wrap-up to **two
  drives**, plus a cloud upload overnight (3-2-1 rule).
- Folder template: `2026-11-18_Fresno-Dinner/{A-Cam,B-Cam,Phone,Audio,Photos}/`.
- Log the timecodes of the strongest moments of the talk in a notes app (it makes the edit about
  three times faster).

## 11. One-camera fallback

If only one camera is available: A-Cam stays locked on the talk (wide enough for Josh and the
screen), with both lavs recording (Lav 2 as a backup onto the transmitter's internal recorder).
The phone on a gimbal covers everything else. Shoot the 4K frame wide and crop in post for
close-ups (4K → 1080p gives a clean 2× punch-in).
